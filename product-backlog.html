Reflection

What happened

Our Product Owner, Tshephiso, was responsible for the backlog. We planned 19 sprints, from the business idea and proposal, through ERD and wireframes, to registration, listings, buyer and seller dashboards, an admin panel, testing, deployment and the final presentation. The sprints were then broken into 109 day-level cards. My part was creating cards on the board and writing some of the user stories.

What it taught me

Day-level cards made the work small and visible. The backlog also changed: the original plan had Sprint 4 as ERD diagrams, but the Sprint 4 day cards cover frontend, backend, database linking and deployment. We were short on time, and our lecturer wanted to see a working product at that point rather than more planning, so we moved into development. Some cards were not kept in step with the work: later sprint-level cards still show no ticked tasks, the Sprint 5 and 6 day cards are empty placeholders, and one card (“Sprint 3 – Day 4”) sits among the Sprint 4 cards.

What I would change

I would keep one backlog in priority order, retire or update cards when the plan changes, and review it at the end of each sprint so it matches the real work. I am taking these lessons into the projects I want to build next: a SaaS that helps small businesses with their finances, and a community web app that works with CPFs.
