Connecting the five artefacts

Our charter set the roles: Tshephiso as Product Owner, Tim as Scrum Master and me as a developer responsible for coding, task breakdown and deployment. The Product Owner’s backlog of 19 sprints became day-level cards with user stories, which became the sprint plans we worked from. The board shows where this worked well (small, visible tasks) and where it slipped (stories written for the team rather than the user, a board updated in bursts, and no written logbook). My biggest lesson is that I should take documentation seriously. I enjoy thinking through a problem and its solution, but I focused on the technical side and neglected the written record. I should also use the sprints properly rather than overworking myself and racing ahead on several sprints before they start.
